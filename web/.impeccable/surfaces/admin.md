# Surface brief — Veyro Admin (web)

Mode: **Operate**. The operator completes a task. Scanability, consistency and the real
usage scene outrank expression; the world lends type, palette, density and one signature
move, never the layout, the navigation model or the controls.

## Direction contract

**THESIS.** This surface owns the morning verdict: what the business did, and the few
things that need a person today, stated at a scale you can read standing up. It refuses
the two arrangements this category always ships — the row of four KPI tiles above a table
on a grey field, and its predictable opposite, the near-black dashboard with a neon accent
and glowing rings. Both treat figures as decoration. Here a figure is the content.

**OWN-WORLD.** The graphic tradition of Levantine football-club identity: flat, fully
saturated colour used as whole fields rather than accents, a bilingual lockup that sets
Arabic and Latin as equals, and the numeral on the back of a shirt. **#91338A owns the
shell and the action** -- one colour for the product's identity and for the one thing on a
page that can be pressed -- with **#26202C** as the ground the dark theme is built on. Bone is
the working ground and ink the text, both carrying the primary's own cast rather than a grey
or a green one. Crimson, gold, steel and indigo are category fields, each a solid block
carrying one kind of concern, and each kept a measured hue distance from the primary so a
state is never mistaken for the brand. Indigo replaced the green here after the Command Center
was rendered and read: a green badge sat above "4 classes have no coach", which told a manager
the thing needing them was fine. Screenprint flatness throughout:
no gradient, no glass, no glow, no rounded-card soup. Rules are 2px and black, not
hairlines. Archivo sets every quantity; Barlow sets Latin text; Cairo sets Arabic.

**STORY.** The operator understands in one look how much came in, how many people came,
and what is unresolved; believes the figures because the product says plainly where each
came from and refuses to invent the ones it cannot derive; and acts by opening the one
record that needs them.

**FIRST VIEWPORT.** A deep-plum rail down the inside edge carrying the bilingual
wordmark and navigation grouped by what the operator is trying to do. To its right, a bone
field. The page opens on a single sentence naming how many things need a person, set large.
Beneath it, the day's quantities as monumental Archivo numerals on a baseline — collected,
members, visits — each with its unit and its provenance in small Barlow beneath, no tiles
and no borders between them. Below that, the queue: one row per unresolved thing, its kind
carried by a solid colour field at the row's head, the amount at risk set as a numeral on
the right, and a single standard link to the record. Primary action sits in the row.

**FORM.** Candidate 7 of 7 on the ordered grounded list, after the invalid seventh (the
gym's own whiteboard and rota wall — the instrument the audience operates, which Operate
mode bars) was replaced by the modernist sports hall, which ranks third. seed:f9d67a95f857351a

**RAISE (from rw-lexicon-shoulder-and-plate, declined).** Colour is quarantined: it
appears only inside a declared region that is doing a job, never scattered across the page.

**RAISE (from digital-design-canon-warm-consumer-app-surface, declined).** The action
colour is reserved strictly for what can actually be acted on; a disabled action loses it.

**RAISE (from vernacular-ephemera-boarding-pass-and-gate-board, competitive).** A row that
changed keeps its identity across a rerank and holds its change marker until it has been seen.

**RAISE (from signals-instruments-split-flap-concourse, declined).** Columns never move;
data changes inside a stable grid.

**RAISE (from notation-diagram-systems-labanotation and terminal-yellow-wayfinding,
competitive).** A magnitude is encoded in a dimension — length or scale — not merely
labelled; and a phone collapses to the single next decision with its figure monumental.

**FINISH.** unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping raster carrying its provenance

## Honest risk

A football-club identity on operations software can read as a sports-team app. The
discipline that prevents it: no crest, no stripe, no mascot, no shirt texture. The world
enters only as type, palette, density and the numeral.

## What the Command Center cannot answer yet

**These were rendered on the screen and are recorded here instead.** The page carried a
"What this screen does not do yet" table of seven notes written for engineers — naming
`ADM-CRM-*`, `MOD-004` and `MOD-014`, and explaining that this surface "renders on the server
and has one client component" — plus a `pace_minor` paragraph and a `by_legal_entity` sentence
under the chart. Every one is a true and useful engineering record and none of them is
something a gym owner can act on or look up. They moved; they were not deleted.

The in-product form of honesty is unchanged and is where it belongs: a figure the build cannot
derive carries a reason in the operator's own language **in the place the figure would have
been**, which is `MetricCell`'s `unavailable` state. One line, no identifiers.

- **Lead conversion over 30 days.** There is no CRM module, so there is no lead, no trial and
  no conversion rate. This is the one note still rendered, reworded: "Veyro is not tracking
  leads yet, so there is no conversion rate to show."
- **The LEADS attention class.** The same module. An uncontacted lead is not a thing this
  build can have.
- **Every primary action the design puts on a queue row** — send the payment links, fix the
  filings, offer the class to another coach — is a stepped-up financial write with no form in
  this app. Each row links to the record instead. Note that this reason is now weaker than it
  was: three screens in this app offer the step-up before the button, so "stepped-up" is no
  longer on its own a reason a form cannot exist. What is missing is the form.
- **The interpretation panel and its evidence links** need a module that reasons about
  revenue. There is none, and the withheld panel is no longer rendered: an operator does not
  need to be told that an absent feature is absent.
- **Collections by club.** The design breaks them down by location; money here carries a legal
  entity, and the hierarchy puts brands and locations under one, so three clubs under a single
  entity are one row. The screen now says this in one operator-language line rather than
  explaining the hierarchy.
- **The period toggle, the resolved-today drawer, the lower-priority collapse, the keyboard
  queue and the 30-second undo** are interactions. This surface renders on the server and has
  one client component.
- **Scope.** The heading says all locations because there is no scope switcher on this screen;
  the gate can answer *where* now, and only `GET /staff` reads that answer.

**Two server response fields now have no reader**, recorded rather than left to be discovered:
`by_location_is_unavailable` and `pace_is_a_projection` on the collections read are prose
written for an engineer, and the backend contract is protected, so they keep being returned
and this surface stops printing them.

## The finish review, and what it left outstanding

Impeccable's finish reviewer audited the build against this contract and measured the captures
pixel by pixel. It **contradicted two claims** that were in my own summary, and it was right on
both: the figures rendered at 44px rather than 72 (a container-query threshold sitting 3px
above the column width the Admin target produces, so `--type-figure-size` had no effective
reader anywhere), and they did not share a baseline (`align-items: end` aligns cell boxes, so
a two-line comparison rode its figure 16px upward). It also found that the category field
carried **severity** rather than kind, so every row wore another category's declared colour and
three of the four `color.category.*` tokens had no reader at all.

Eight material fixes were returned. Six are done and verified in the browser:

1. The figure row is a flex line, so figures size to their own content and the prose cell
   absorbs the rest. Measured: 72px, three figures, tops at 148/148/148.
2. The chip carries kind. Money is gold, compliance steel, retention crimson, staffing indigo,
   each with the text colour that clears AA on it in both themes -- in dark every category is
   a light tint, so all four take ink there.
3. The dark rail was **1.00:1** against the dark canvas; no value in the shell's ramp separates
   from a canvas of its own family by luminance, so the field takes the world's own 2px rule.
4. The money amount in the queue was a red floating outside any region, and two rules set two
   different reds on it, resolved by specificity. It is ink, as the metric row's own note
   requires: "the metrics do not turn red -- they are still facts".
5. `brightness(0.95)` on ink.900 is #0c110f, which is no hover state at all on the page's
   primary action. `color.action.hover` is a real step.
6. Arabic-Indic digits typed into four message values rendered beside Latin figures on the
   same screen, and `cc.staffingBody` did not pass the tenant's numbering. A catalogue-digit
   sweep now covers what the existing numerals checks are blind to, and it found two of the
   four itself, on a different screen.

## Still owed on this surface

- The gap-note section pattern survives on other screens (`/schedule` renders one). The same
  treatment is owed there: the records move, the in-product line stays.
- The bottom nav at phone width is one scrolling strip of every section rather than the five
  the breakpoints specify. A disclosure holding the rest is the real answer and is a component
  this build does not have.
- The chrome wraps to three lines at 375px, because the language and theme switches are six
  controls in a row. It costs about 110px of a phone viewport.
- **The phone's first viewport is chrome and metrics, and no queue row is visible.** Measured
  at 375x812: 116px, 14% of the viewport, is spent on the language and theme switches above
  the verdict, and the queue's heading lands at y703 with its first row below the fold. RAISE 5
  and bp.xs both say the surface collapses to the single next decision. `.ds-chrome`'s
  `flex-wrap` is the mechanism, and the fix is a disclosure for the locale and theme controls.
- **Spacing is off the 4px scale in the two components this surface rests on** -- 5, 7, 9, 11,
  13, 15 and 15.5px across the queue row and the chrome, and `font-size: 15px` on the row
  headline is not a type-scale step. The CSS comments say these were read off the retired
  canvas's own HTML, so the new world inherited the old one's grid.
- **The bilingual lockup is the one OWN-WORLD ingredient not built.** The mark swaps per
  locale -- "V | Veyro" in English, "ف | فيرو" in Arabic -- rather than setting both scripts as
  equals in one lockup, which is what CHARACTER's "bilingual by construction" names.
- **`type.display` has no reader.** The opening verdict sentence is an `h1` at 28px, so it is
  smaller than the figures beneath it; the 40px register the system declares for exactly that
  sentence is unused.
- **Archivo is used as a text face in four places in the queue row** (`-category`, `-when`,
  `-meaning`, `-owner`) where FACES assigns Latin text to Barlow.
- **The hero figure.** The metric row is four peers at the 44px register, because four at 72
  do not fit a 1152px content area -- the arithmetic is in `DESIGN.md`. The composition the
  contract's FIRST VIEWPORT really wants is the collected figure at 72 on its own line, with
  members, visits and the lead cell secondary beneath it: a real hierarchy rather than four
  equal peers, and it fits because a single figure gets the whole width. That is a change to
  `MetricRow`'s API, which assumes peers, so it is the next step rather than this slice.
- The collections chart has one non-zero week in the fixture, so the bar scale is proven but
  the chart demonstrates little. A fixture with a real distribution would show whether the
  bars read as a series.


## The primary, replaced on 2026-11-26

The owner named **#91338A** as the product's primary and **#26202C** as the ground for dark
mode, replacing the green this surface shipped with. What moved and what did not:

- **The hue of everything derived.** `plum` is built from #91338A's own hue and carries the
  shell field, links, selection and the action; `aubergine` is built from #26202C's and
  carries the dark canvas, panels, overlays and rail. The neutrals were re-cast from green to
  the primary's family, so a page with no colour on it still reads as this product.
- **The action stopped being ink.** Guardrail G2 had forced that: the shell and success were
  one green, so a saturated action would have shared a hue with a status. The primary shares a
  hue with nothing, so it is the shell field *and* the pressable colour. This is the one place
  where the owner's choice made the system better rather than only different.
- **Error moved to a true red and AI to an indigo.** The retired error was a claret 37 degrees
  from the new primary and the AI violet was 57 -- close enough that a refusal or an inference
  would have read as the brand. They are 51 and 73 now.
- **Green survives in one job: success.** A status is not an identity, and a paid invoice in
  the brand colour is a highlight rather than a state. The staffing category shared green's
  ramp when this was written and no longer does -- the sentence was true of the rule and false
  of the build until somebody looked at a screen against it, which is the cheapest instrument
  this surface has and the one that was not used for a week.
- **The structure moved afterwards, and not with the palette.** The type scale was rebuilt
  around a monumental figure (88px) with the body at 16px and a navigation label at 15px
  rather than 13; the four density modes grew a step; every screen that counts something now
  leads with the count. Those are answers to "the fonts are small and the UI is boring", not
  to the colour. What the palette change itself moved: same signature move,
  same craft floor. Every contrast pairing was recomputed from the shipped hexes before it was
  written down, and the one that got tighter -- warning on the darkest ground -- was corrected
  rather than tolerated.

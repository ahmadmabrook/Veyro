P0-4 · NUTRITION CLINICAL POLICY — ARCHITECTURE, NOT CRITERIA
I have withdrawn the seven-condition trigger list from D2. It was a designer’s guess on a clinical question and should never have been specified here.
What replaces it is a policy engine the UX does not depend on. Veyro ships a review-routing mechanism with an empty rule set; the rule set is authored per market by whoever holds clinical accountability, and it can change after launch without touching a single screen. See 3g for the surface. Three properties make this safe:

· The coach UX is identical whether zero rules or thirty rules exist. Assignment either proceeds or routes; the builder, the meal cards and the assign action never change shape.
· Rules are data, not layout. A market adding a condition adds a row in a governed policy table — no wireframe, no release, no design review.
· The unconfigured state is explicit, not silent. A tenant in a market with no published policy sees that state at onboarding and cannot quietly operate as though the question were settled.

Veyro’s product responsibility is that a review can be required, routed, evidenced and audited. What must be reviewed is a clinical and regulatory determination and is out of design scope.

RECOMMENDATION · COMMAND CENTER
Take 1a as the structure, absorb 1b’s brief as a three-line header, and make 1c a scope behaviour rather than a separate screen. Drawn as 2a.
The Blueprint is explicit that the first screen answers what happened, what needs attention, why, and what to do — and that exceptions come before charts. Only 1a makes the queue the primary object, and it is the only one of the three where the remedy sits on the row that raised the problem.

1b was not chosen but is not discarded. Its narrative genuinely answers “why” better than a list of counts, and Blueprint INT-02 requires explanation with evidence. But as the page structure it fails two ways: it makes the actionable column narrow at exactly the moment the user wants to act, and it makes an AI outage a structural failure rather than a degraded feature (§UX-R5). Demoted to a collapsible three-line header with underlined evidence, and to the full brief on demand.

1c was not chosen but earns a condition. Grouping by Money / People / Operations with an owner per card is the right shape when the user’s scope is more than one club and their job is to delegate rather than resolve — a regional manager with nine exceptions across three clubs. Rather than a second screen, the queue groups by domain and shows an owner column when scope resolves to multiple locations. Same components, same page, different composition — which is also what keeps this from becoming two products to maintain at 50 locations.
RECOMMENDATION · MEMBER 360
Hybrid of 1f’s shell and 1d’s spine. Reject 1e. Drawn as 2b.
1e’s twelve-item rail is precisely the failure the Foundation flagged as UX-R2: the most important screen in the product becomes a filing cabinet, and “what happened with this member” — the question staff actually ask — is one of twelve equal doors.

1f wins the frame because real admin work is queue work: fourteen expiring memberships, six failed payments, eleven at-risk members. Losing the list to open a profile and losing the profile to return to the list is the single biggest tax on a manager’s day, and it gets worse at 100,000 members, not better.

1d wins the content model: the timeline is the spine, not a tab, and state lives in a fixed left column so orientation never depends on which section is open. The hybrid keeps the list, puts overview + timeline side by side in the inspector, and keeps every section reachable without leaving either. BLOCKING BLUEPRINT AMBIGUITIES — RECOMMENDED PRODUCT DECISIONS
Each of these changed a screen in this round. Recommendation, rationale, UX consequence and business or safety implication — none silently assumed.
D1 · COM-03 / OPS-03Does an unpaid balance block physical access in V1?
Recommend: no hard block in V1. Arrears never stops a turnstile. Instead a soft stop at the desk — reception sees the balance and a one-tap resolution, the member sees nothing at the gate. A tenant-configurable hard block exists but ships off, and when enabled cannot trigger before day 14 of the dunning ladder and never on a first failure.
Why. A refused turnstile in a busy lobby is a public humiliation for a JOD 45 card expiry, and the Blueprint’s own rule is that recognition failure uses neutral language with immediate fallback. Recovery data across subscription businesses is consistent: the embarrassment converts anger, not payment. UX impact: Front Desk needs a discreet balance affordance and inline collection (drawn in 2i); Member app must surface the failure before arrival, not at it. Business implication: slightly slower recovery on hard decliners, offset by fewer involuntary cancellations and fewer desk arguments — measure recovery rate and involuntary churn together, never separately.
D2 · COA-03 / COA-05Where is the line between coach-authored nutrition and qualified review?
Recommend: coaches author freely inside a defined envelope; hard triggers route to qualified review before assignment, in V1 — not Enterprise. Triggers: under 18, pregnancy or breastfeeding, diagnosed diabetes or insulin use, disordered-eating signal, cardiac or renal condition on file, prescribed weight medication, or a target below 1,200 kcal. AI may draft inside the envelope; AI never publishes, and AI never authors for a triggered client.
Why. Deferring the review queue to Enterprise means V1 tenants have no path for exactly the clients where harm is possible, and the Blueprint states Veyro does not diagnose or treat. A trigger list is enforceable in UI; “use judgement” is not. UX impact: the nutrition builder needs a visible scope banner and a blocked-assign state with a named reviewer (drawn in 2m); a tenant with no qualified reviewer configured sees the trigger at onboarding, not at assign time. Safety implication: this is the highest-liability surface in the product — the trigger list needs clinical sign-off per launch market before hi-fi, and it must be configurable upward but never downward by a tenant.
D3 · IAM-02Is facial gym entry ever mandatory, and where is it available?
Recommend: never mandatory for a member, ever. Off by default per tenant, gated by a jurisdiction allow-list, and QR always remains a first-class equal path — not a fallback. Corporate and franchise contracts may not make enrolment a condition of membership.
Why. Biometric law differs sharply across MENA/GCC and the EU, and consent obtained as a condition of entry is not consent. UX impact: enrolment is a deliberate, revocable opt-in in the Identity Center with retention stated in plain language; no screen may present QR as the degraded option. Business implication: hardware sold as a differentiator cannot be sold as a compliance shortcut; Veyro carries the jurisdiction allow-list as product configuration, not as legal advice in a contract.
D4 · B2B-01Who owns the membership when a sponsored employee leaves the employer?
Recommend: 30-day grace, member-visible from day one, defaulting to conversion — not cancellation. On leaver sync the member keeps full access for 30 days, sees the change and the personal price on their first app open, and converts with one action. Employer billing stops at the leave date; Veyro absorbs no revenue silently.
Why. Silent expiry produces a denied entry weeks later with no explanation — the worst version of D1. UX impact: Member app needs a sponsored-membership state with a countdown and a price; Admin needs a leaver queue on the Command Center. Business implication: corporate churn becomes a retail conversion opportunity with a measurable rate, and the employer is never invoiced past the leave date.
D5 · OPS-05What compensation data can a Club Manager see?
Recommend: commission accrual and sales/session performance for their own location’s staff — yes. Salary, contract terms, bank detail, deductions and payroll export — Accountant and Owner only. Commission is operational performance data; salary is protected HR data.
UX impact: the Team area composes differently by role rather than greying rows — a manager never sees a payroll section they cannot open (principle 07). Business implication: managers can coach on commission without HR exposure; disputes route to the Accountant with an audit trail.
D6 · ENG-01WhatsApp initiation outside the 24-hour window.
Recommend: the constraint is shown in the composer, not discovered on send. Inside the window, staff type freely. Outside it, the composer switches to approved templates with the reason stated in one line and the free-text box disabled with an explanation — never a silent failure after send.
UX impact: one composer with two states rather than two features. Business implication: template library becomes an onboarding deliverable per language, and message cost stays predictable.
D7 · B2B-02Cross-location members: who owns revenue, coaching and data?
Recommend: home club owns the membership revenue, the coaching relationship and the member record. The visited club receives utilization credit and cost allocation, not revenue. Home club is set at sale, member-visible, and changeable only as an explicit transfer with both managers notified.
Why. Any other split makes branch comparison meaningless and turns franchise scorecards into an argument. UX impact: Member 360 shows home club in the identity header; branch scorecards show revenue and utilization as separate columns. Business implication: royalty and intercompany allocation have a defensible basis; needs franchise-agreement sign-off before Enterprise build.

RTL decisions
Numerals are a tenant setting
Arabic-Indic or Latin, applied identically on screen, receipt and export. Never mixed within one tenant.
Money stays isolated
“JOD 312” is a bidi-isolated run so the currency code never jumps to the wrong side of the number.
Phone numbers stay LTR
+962 79 555 0134 reads left-to-right inside Arabic text, always.
Timeline flows right-to-left
The spine moves to the right edge and time runs right→left. Mirroring the container but not the axis is the classic bug.
Progress is not flipped blindly
Set counters and week progress mirror. Charts with a time axis keep time running right→left; charts of magnitude do not mirror at all.
Exercise names stay bilingual
Arabic label plus the Latin lift name — gym-floor vocabulary in Amman is mixed, and translating “bench press” only causes confusion.
Long strings
Arabic runs 20–30% longer here. Every label in this set is drawn at the longer length, not the English one.

PHASE 9 · ARABIC RTL · COACH & MEMBER

المدرب والعضو

The two hardest Arabic problems in the product sit here: a set logger where weight, multiplication and reps form one mathematical expression, and a member app where a machine-translated tone would do more damage than any layout bug.

THE SET LOGGER DECISION
“75.0 kg × 5” is one expression and stays LTR as a whole — not three mirrored fragments. Mirroring it gives “5 × kg 75.0”, which reads as five sets of something rather than five reps at a weight. The expression sits in a single isolated run at the logical start of the row, and the set number and Log button take the logical end. Arabic-Indic numerals are wrong here too: a barbell is loaded in Latin-marked plates and coaches say “سبعين كيلو” while reading 70.
THE MEMBER TONE DECISION
Written in Levantine-inflected MSA — the register a Jordanian gym would actually use in writing. “بطاقتك انتهت” not “انتهت صلاحية بطاقتك المصرفية”. Second person masculine for Ahmad throughout, because Arabic forces a gender choice the English never surfaced, and the member's gender is on the record. Guessing wrong here is worse than any contrast failure.

THE EXPRESSION STAYS WHOLE
The English canvas split weight, “kg ×” and reps into three separately-aligned boxes so the columns stacked. In Arabic that produces a mirrored fragment order, so the three merge into one isolated LTR run — 75.0 kg × 5 — and column alignment is achieved by the run itself being start-aligned. The set number and Log button swap to the logical ends. This is a real layout change, not a mirror.
WEIGHTS AND REPS ARE LATIN, TIMES ARE NOT
Barbell plates are stamped in Latin numerals, and a coach reading a plan aloud says the Arabic word while looking at 70. Set indices, clock times, durations and rest timers do take Arabic-Indic, because they are ordinary counts. The tenant numeral setting governs the second group and not the first — an exception the money rule already established, applied to physical measurement.
ACTIVE-SET WASH USES LOGICAL BORDER
The accent bar on set 3 is border-inline-start, so it lands on the right in Arabic beside where the eye enters the row. Nothing in the component knows which direction it is in — which is the whole point of the token contract.
MEASURED
Time column 48 vs 44 · nav labels fit at 10.5px Arabic without truncation · “وثّق الـ٢٢ كلها” is 8% wider than “Confirm all 22” and the button was sized for it · the voice-note block gained one line at 1.85 line-height. Frame stays 390 — nothing needed a wider phone.

REGISTER, NOT TRANSLATION
“دفعة آب ما مرّت” is how a person says a payment failed — the formal “لم تنجح عملية الدفع” is bank language and lands as a threat. “رتّبها وقت ما يناسبك” carries the same permission as “sort it whenever”. The food names are Levantine (الغدا, شوربة عدس, رغيفين), not MSA textbook forms.
MACROS SPELLED OUT, NOT ABBREVIATED
The English canvas uses 52P 74C 18F — a compact Latin convention with no Arabic equivalent. Arabic gets “بروتين ٥٢ · كارب ٧٤ · دهون ١٨”, which is longer and forced the meal card taller. Inventing Arabic single-letter abbreviations would have been shorter and unreadable.
DIRECTIONAL DETAILS
The macro bar fills from the right. The “ate something else” affordance mirrors to ←. The sheet still rises from the bottom — vertical motion has no direction to mirror, and a sheet that slid in from the side would be a different component, not a localised one.
GENDER IS A DATA DEPENDENCY
“غبتَ”, “بتقدر”, “تتدرّب” are all masculine second-person. Arabic makes every sentence to a member a gendered sentence — so member gender becomes a rendering input, not a profile detail, and a missing value must fall back to a neutral construction rather than guessing. Filed as a content requirement for every Arabic member-facing string in the product.

Veyro Design System — visual direction

Three directions that differ structurally, not chromatically — different density, type, surface model, border logic, radius and data treatment. Each is shown as a spec panel plus the same three specimens: a Command Center attention row, a member identity row with money, and a Member meal card. Same content, three visual languages, so the comparison is honest. Foundations, tokens and components come after a direction is chosen — per §6 of the brief, no polished screens yet.

PHILOSOPHY
The interface is a ledger, and a ledger earns trust by being legible rather than styled. Every boundary is a hairline rule; nothing floats. Money and counts are set in mono so columns align without effort. Warm paper greys keep eight hours of table-reading from feeling clinical.

1b Signal — structure from surface. Layered greys instead of borders, radius on anything interactive, one accent reserved strictly for action and selection.
PHILOSOPHY
Depth replaces outlines. Elements separate because they sit on different planes, so a dense table reads as one calm field instead of a grid of boxes. Radius is a signal of interactivity, not decoration: if it curves, you can act on it. Exactly one accent, spent only on action and selection.

RECOMMENDATION
Build on 1a Ledger, absorb two things from 1b Signal, and take one idea from 1c Calm. Reject 1c as a base.
Veyro's centre of gravity is operational: Admin, Front Desk and the Console are where the hours are spent and where the money is. 1a is the only direction whose structure improves as density increases — hairline rules and mono numerals get better at 40 rows, while shadows and pills get worse. It is also the only one with a superfamily that covers Latin, Arabic and mono as one designed system, which matters more than any other single decision given that Arabic is half the launch market and a competitor's Arabic build is usually its weakest surface.
Absorb from 1b: radius as an interactivity signal
Ledger's near-zero radius stays on data and containers, but anything pressable gets a 6–8px radius. It gives touch surfaces the affordance Ledger lacks without softening a single table.
Absorb from 1b: one accent, spent only on action
Ink blue #1b4079 for action and selection exclusively; semantic colour lives only inside status components. Adjacency between accent and semantic green is prohibited by rule, not avoided by taste.
Take from 1c: an editorial register for member moments
Not the serif — the scale. Member first-run, milestones, empty states and progress reviews get a display size and generous rhythm the operational surfaces never use. Same family, different voice.
Reject from 1c: the serif and the shared hue
No credible Arabic serif companion exists for UI, so the identity would evaporate on half the product. Action and success sharing a hue is an accessibility defect, not a stylistic choice.
The result is one token set with four density modes — Compact (Admin tables, Console), Default (Admin detail, CRM), Touch (Coach, Front Desk), Spacious (Member) — where density changes row height, spacing scale and type step, and nothing else. Same components, same tokens, four compositions. That is what keeps Front Desk from becoming a third build and what makes the Member app a breakpoint rather than a separate design language.
TWO FLAGS, PER §2 OF THE BRIEF
1 · Severity is currently expressed twice. Nothing here reopens approved UX architecture, but building the specimens surfaced a gap the wireframes left implicit: the attention row's severity edge-mark and the status badge are two different mechanisms for one idea, and a reader can reasonably take them as independent signals. Recommend one severity scale used by both — edge-mark for the row-level roll-up, badge always naming the specific state. Resolved in the status component, not on any screen.
2 · The secondary text ramp failed AA in all three directions, and has been corrected. The first pass of these specimens used light greys for member IDs, branch names and timestamps that measured between 2.34:1 and 4.12:1 — below the 4.5:1 required under 18.66px. Every ramp above is now darkened to pass on its own surface. This matters more than it looks: those greys become color.text.secondary and color.text.tertiary in Step 2, so had they been adopted as drawn they would have propagated into every surface in the product — precisely the retrofit-after-hi-fi failure that VALIDATE-1 exists to prevent. Rule carried into Step 2: the light end of the neutral ramp is available to large text and non-essential decoration only; any token used for text under 18.66px must be contrast-verified against every surface it may sit on, and the token set ships with that matrix rather than an assurance.

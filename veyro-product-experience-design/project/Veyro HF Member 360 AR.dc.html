Member 360 — العربية

The timeline is where RTL gets genuinely difficult, and the reason this screen exists as a full canvas rather than a note. A timeline has two directions at once — layout direction and the arrow of time — and they are not the same axis.

THE DECISION THAT MATTERS ON THIS SCREEN
The timeline mirrors horizontally but time still runs downward. Timestamps and the spine move to the right edge, the text flows leftward from them — but newest stays at the top. Time in a vertical list is not a directional property of the script; it is a convention every Arabic reader already holds from every messaging app they use. Flipping it would be mirroring for its own sake, which Foundations explicitly warns against.

MIXED NAMES, SORTED AND SEARCHABLE
Numerals follow one rule shared with the Command Center: money and Latin identifiers stay Plex Mono LTR-isolated, everything else takes the tenant numeral setting in Plex Sans Arabic with tabular figures. Separately, the roster holds “أحمد النابلسي” and “Ahmad Nabulsi” as one record with both spellings indexed — a receptionist typing either finds him, and the list sorts by the tenant's collation rather than by codepoint. Latin-named members in an Arabic tenant render in Plex Sans and stay LTR within an otherwise RTL row.
PLAN NAMES ARE TRANSLITERATED, NOT TRANSLATED
Club becomes كلب and Club+ becomes كلب بلس, because they are product names the member sees on their contract — translating them to نادي would create two names for one product across app, receipt and invoice. Flex is فليكس on the same reasoning.
WHAT THE ARABIC BUILD COST IN PIXELS
List column 276 against 250 · timestamp column 70 against 66 · row height 56 against 52 · metric tiles a minimum 158 against 150. All from line-height 1.75–1.9, the 13px floor, and Arabic labels running longer. The layout was measured in Arabic and the English canvas fits comfortably inside it — that is the correct direction to size in, not the reverse.

PHASE 9 · ARABIC RTL · FRONT DESK & POS

الاستقبال ونقطة البيع

The POS cart is the hardest RTL surface in Veyro — a column of quantities, names and amounts where three different directional rules collide in every row. It was measured rather than mirrored, and the measurement changed the layout.

THE CART DECISION
The amount column stays at the logical end and the quantity at the logical start — so in Arabic, quantity is rightmost and money leftmost, the reverse of the English screen. What does not reverse is the alignment inside the money column: amounts stay end-aligned to each other as an LTR block, because a column of numbers that agree on their decimal point is the entire reason for tabular figures. Mirroring the alignment as well would have produced a cart where 1.00 and 55.00 no longer line up.

CART · WHAT MOVED AND WHAT DID NOT
Moved: quantity to the right, amounts to the left, cart width 250→288, the tax note onto two lines, and the total now carries JOD explicitly because “٦٠٫٧٢” alone in an Arabic total line is ambiguous about currency in a way the English “60.72” under a JOD subtotal is not.
Did not move: amount alignment. Each amount is an isolated LTR run with min-width and end-alignment inside its own box, so the column agrees on its decimals. Tabular figures exist for exactly this, and mirroring the alignment would have destroyed it.
QUANTITY NUMERALS ARE ARABIC-INDIC
٢× and ١× follow the tenant numeral setting — they are counts, not money. Prices and totals keep Latin digits with the code, because they appear on the receipt, the invoice and the JoFotara submission, and a member disputing a charge must read the same string in all four places. The rule is one line long and it decides every numeral in the product.
CLIQ IS كليك, NOT A TRANSLATION
The Jordanian instant-payment scheme is spoken as “CliQ” and written كليك in Arabic-language banking material. Translating it to a descriptive phrase would leave a receptionist looking for a button that does not match what the customer just said out loud.

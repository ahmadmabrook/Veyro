ORDERING IS A COMPONENT CONCERN
Sorted by severity, then money at risk, then age — never by recency alone. A three-hour-old JOD 312 failure outranks a five-minute-old staffing note, and the queue must not reshuffle while someone is reading it: new items land in a “3 new” pill at the top rather than injecting themselves.
STATES
Empty is the success state — “Nothing needs you at Khalda. Last checked 14:20.” with the day's numbers below, never a blank panel. Resolving collapses the row in place with a 30s undo, and the count decrements visibly. Snoozed items show where they went. Permission-filtered queues state that items are hidden without saying what they are.
CONTRACTS
Each row is an article with the headline as its label; the queue is a list with a live count. Severity is edge-mark and category chip and wording — never the bar alone. RTL: edge-mark moves to the logical start, amount to the logical end. Below bp.sm the action row stacks and the primary becomes full-width.

WHY NO “TYPE THE AMOUNT TO CONFIRM”
Retyping proves dexterity, not intent — people copy the number without reading it. The real controls are stating the consequence in full, putting the amount in the button, requiring a reason that becomes part of the record, and holding a refund permission. Above a tenant threshold it escalates to approval rather than to more typing.

ONE-TAP IS THE WHOLE DESIGN
Both components pre-fill from the last known value and make the common case a single 44px tap. SetLogger never asks for weight twice in a session; MealCard never asks a member to search for food that is already on their plan. Adjustment is available but never required — that is the difference between logging and data entry.
OFFLINE IS THE NORMAL CASE
Basements have no signal. Both write locally and mark themselves “saved on this phone · will sync”, with the count of pending items visible and never a blocking error. A failed sync surfaces once, at the app level, with a retry — not per set.
PROGRESSCHART · HONEST BY DEFAULT
Never a truncated y-axis, never a trend line through fewer than four points, and gaps rendered as gaps rather than interpolated. Direction is stated in words as well as slope — “down 1.8 kg over 6 weeks” — because a line going down is good for weight and bad for strength, and colour cannot carry that.

FIVE RULES, NO EXCEPTIONS
1 · Always marked. The ✦ and the color.semantic.ai frame appear wherever a machine produced the content — no unattributed AI text anywhere in Veyro.
5 · Always Veyro's colour. ai.600 is system-owned and never inherits brand.accent (guardrail G7). A tenant brand on an AI panel would make inference look like a first-party assertion; Veyro is accountable for what its AI says, so Veyro owns the colour it says it in. Applies to interpretation, recommendation, evidence links and executed-action confirmations alike.
2 · Always evidenced. Every claim carries a link to the records behind it. A number without a route to its source is not shippable.
3 · Never auto-executes. Output is a proposal; a human presses the button. Financial and destructive actions are excluded entirely.
4 · States its confidence in words, not a percentage — “lower on the roster attribution” is actionable, “72%” is not.
NUTRITION CLINICAL GATE · CONFIGURABLE, NOT DECIDED
Per your instruction, Veyro defines no clinical criteria. The architecture provides a policy routing point: a plan passes through nutritionPolicy.evaluate() and lands in one of four outcomes — auto-assign, coach review, qualified-professional review, or blocked with a referral. The rules populating it are a market-specific compliance input, owned outside the product.
Until a market publishes rules, its tenants run in review-all or nutrition-disabled. The UX does not change when rules arrive — only which branch a plan takes. That was the requirement: configurable without redesign.
